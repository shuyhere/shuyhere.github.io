About me

Shu Yang is a Ph.D. student in Computer Science at King Abdullah University of Science and Technology (KAUST), advised by Prof. Di Wang in the PRADA Lab (Provable Responsible AI and Data Analytics Lab). She received her M.Sc. in Computational Linguistics from the University of Macau, advised by Prof. Derek F. Wong. She has been a visiting scholar at the University of Edinburgh and Tsinghua University, and is currently a Visiting Student Researcher at Stanford HAI. Her research interests lie in responsible AI, large language models, and computational linguistics. She is one of the organizers of the First Workshop on LLM Persona Modeling at NeurIPS 2025.

My research is centered on advancing the trustworthiness and alignment of large language models. I tackle key challenges in AI safety and explainability, with a particular emphasis on two areas. First, I investigate emergent deceptive behaviors such as specification gaming, reward hacking, and sycophancy, aiming to understand their underlying mechanisms. Second, I leverage these insights to develop LLMs that are not only more aligned with complex human intent but are also designed for more effective monitoring and supervision, ensuring that their expressed reasoning accurately reflects their internal states.

Featured: Model Autophagy Analysis to Explicate Self-consumption within Human-AI Interactions

Shu Yang*, Muhammad Asif Ali, Lu Yu, Lijie Hu, Di Wang · CoLM 2024 OpenReview
  • A progressive prevalence of model-generated synthetic information over time within training datasets compared to human-generated information.
  • The discernible tendency of large models, when acting as information transmitters across multiple iterations, to selectively modify or prioritize specific contents.
  • The potential for a reduction in the diversity of socially or human-generated information, leading to bottlenecks in model performance and confining models to local optima.
Keywords: XAI, self-consumption, human–AI interactions
